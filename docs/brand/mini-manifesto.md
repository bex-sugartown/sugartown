## The whole thing in six lines

Content is infrastructure, not a page.
Composable is a set of contracts, not a shopping list.
AI belongs in the workflow, not the press release.
Migration is a program with a schedule, not a cutover with a prayer.
Design tokens and content types are the same problem wearing different job titles.
If you cannot govern it, you do not own it.

## Five positions

### 1. Cloud-first is settled. The hard part moved.

SaaS won the delivery argument. Nobody is buying elasticity any more, they are buying whether the thing survives contact with legal, procurement, and a regional data rule nobody mentioned in the demo. The interesting questions are all downstream of the hosting decision now.

### 2. Composable is a set of contracts

An API-first platform does not make a stack composable. What makes it composable is knowing which system owns which field, and what happens when one of them is down. Buyers who negotiate portability get modular architecture. Buyers who buy a marketplace get a bill.

### 3. AI in the workflow, not the announcement

Generative and agentic features are worth paying for when they shorten cycle time, raise reuse, and leave an audit trail. Pilots demo well. Programs need guardrails, provenance, and a number that moved. The AI is a tool, not a teammate, and humans stay accountable for what ships.

### 4. Migration is a program, not a cutover

Every replatform is really a URL, SEO, and integration problem wearing a CMS costume. Staged, instrumented, reversible, boring: that is what good looks like. The heroic weekend cutover is a story people tell because the alternative does not make a good story.

### 5. Design systems and content types are the same problem

A token that nothing consumes and a content type that nothing renders fail the same way. Structured content, design tokens, and analytics have to agree on what a thing is called before anyone can ship faster. Visual assembly and live preview are how authors stop resenting headless discipline.
