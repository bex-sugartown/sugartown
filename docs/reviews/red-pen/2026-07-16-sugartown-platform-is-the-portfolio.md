## Editor's verdict

This is a strong, on-voice piece — the strongest kind of case study Sugartown can publish, because the central claim (agnostic architecture, governed AI) is backed by receipts that check out against the repo, not just asserted. Every headline number I could verify against source (23 document types, 11 section blocks, 60/63/14 taxonomy counts, 26 migrated tables, 15 architecture decision records, the "two packaging gaps") landed exactly on the nose. That's rare and it's worth naming as a strength before anything else.

The weak link is bookkeeping, not writing: one number (656 tokens) has drifted stale since publish because a later commit deleted dead tokens, and the geoSummary field claims a validator count that doesn't match the repo. Neither is a voice problem — the prose itself is clean, on-register, and needs almost no line-editing. The one real craft opportunity is structural: the fig metaphor is the most memorable writing in the piece and it's confined to a single section that neither the title nor the closer ever calls back to. Publishable as-is today; tightening the two stale numbers before the next read-through is the only urgent item.

## Narrative map

**Thesis:** A platform is a more credible portfolio than a description of one — because both halves of the claim that matter (the architecture survives a vendor switch, and the AI that builds it can't go rogue) are tested facts, not assertions.

**Beats:** Hook (Challenge callout: portfolios that only claim competence are a liability) → proof-up-front (Receipts tiles) → thematic aside (the fig) → deep narrative: tension (WordPress blob, no separation of content/theme) → role → escalation (model first, migrate, test the real fear, govern, close the loop) → resolution (Key Outcomes) → aftermath (Reflection) → FAQ. This follows the case-study template's canonical order (Challenge callout → outcome tiles → Overview → FAQ) — the apparent duplication of "Challenge" and "Key Outcomes" (once as a top-level callout/tiles, again inside Overview) is the house pattern: skim layer up top, deep-read layer below. Working as intended.

**Skim skeleton:** Title ("The Platform Is the Portfolio") → eyebrow ("Case Study") → Challenge callout's two opening sentences ("A portfolio that only claims senior platform competence is a liability. I wanted the opposite...") → four Receipts tile headlines (Vendor agnostic architecture / AI drafts, a human always approves / Structured pieces, not one big blob / One system, no duplicate tags) → "The Platform Is a Fig" (heading, no lead-in from the title) → Overview / Challenge / My Role / Process / Key Outcomes / Reflection subheads → closer ("Building it in the correct order is boring right up until it is the reason the thing holds.") → Key Questions. A skimmer gets the thesis and the proof points cleanly. The one discontinuity: "The Platform Is a Fig" arrives with zero setup at skim depth — the title never signals a fig is coming, so a skim-only reader hits a fruit metaphor cold between two more literal sections.

**Length:** ~1,590 words of body copy (Challenge + fig section + Overview + FAQ + outcome tiles). No stated word band exists for case studies specifically, but this sits comfortably inside the range the brand guide documents for articles (peak engagement ~1,600 words) — no section reads as padded, no debt to pay down.

## What worked

- **The receipts are actually receipts.** Every hard number in the piece that I could check against the repo — 23 document types, 11 composable section blocks, 60 tools / 63 tags / 14 categories, 26 migrated tables, 15 architecture decision records — matched exactly. For a case study whose entire thesis is "agnosticism is worthless as a claim and valuable only as a tested fact," having the numbers actually be tested facts is the piece walking its own talk.
- **The comedic/directive contract holds throughout.** "I pointed Claude Code at the problem... not hand-fixed" is the textbook Bex-directs/AI-builds pattern from the voice guide, used correctly every time credit for building comes up. Not one instance of Bex claiming to have written code herself.
- **"Test the real lock-in fear, not the easy one."** Sharp, specific, and it does real argumentative work — it pre-empts the obvious objection (a design system rendering twice proves nothing) before a skeptical reader can raise it.
- **The AI disclosure line.** "Fig metaphor by D.H. Lawrence (& Ken Russell)" is a genuinely good touch — specific, a little cheeky, and accurate (Russell's *Women in Love* is the famous screen adaptation of the fig monologue). This is exactly the kind of specific, earned detail the credibility-without-chest-thumping principle asks for.

## Notes to the writer (no edit proposed)

- The thesis works but is never stated in one sentence anywhere in the piece — worth deciding whether that's intentional (let the receipts speak) or worth a single explicit line somewhere in Overview.
- The fig section's placement (between the Receipts tiles and Overview) is unusual but not wrong — it's doing thematic/tonal work the more literal sections don't, and removing it would flatten the piece. The finding above is about connecting it to the rest, not about whether it belongs.

## Unverifiable claims

None. Every checkable factual claim in the body copy and metadata was checked against the repo, live Sanity content, or shipped epic docs (see Findings and verdict above for the two that came back stale/wrong).
