## Editor's verdict

Publishable once the title is settled and the four accuracy rows are taken. The thesis is clear, the voice is Bex's, and the best passage (the "Not 'no PIM'" paragraph in the Salsify section) is the piece's argument in three beats. The weak link is the middle: five consecutive sections about what transfers (table, semantic naming, journey, diagram, proof of concept) before the stakes return at the Salsify case, which is the strongest evidence and sits about 70% of the way through. Three sentences claim more than the piece can prove ("solve the same problems", "the learning was the cheap part", a heading that says what a filter "actually measures" while the body says "I can't see inside the filter"). One sentence breaks the article voice contract ("I once built a proof of concept").

## Narrative map

**Thesis:** Job posts that name a platform instead of the work screen out people whose skills transfer, because platforms are adjacent and the habits underneath carry across them.

**Beats:** hook and stakes (turned down for roles I am qualified for) → thesis (a tool name is a proxy) → evidence 1 (table, semantic naming) → evidence 2 (my own journey, two-journey diagram) → evidence 3 (proof of concept) → counter-case with a stated reason (Salsify) → mechanism, labelled a theory (keyword filter) → resolution (what to write in the job description) → payoff (callback closer). Nothing is missing. The Salsify case is out of order: it is the escalation beat but arrives after five evidence beats.

**Skim skeleton:**
- Title: Platforms Are Adjacent. The Keyword Filter Doesn't Know That.
- TL;DR: Job posts now ask for a named platform where they used to ask for a skill...
- The job post asks for a logo, not a skill: "Months into a job search, I have started reading requirements sections the way I read bug reports: for the pattern."
- A tool name is a proxy, and a bad one: "Three years in Platform X" is shorthand.
- Same job, different nouns: "Here is one job in three platform types."
- How I learned it: Photoshop, then the web, then the DAM: "This is how I actually learned it, one platform at a time." / closer: "Each stop changed what I was doing."
- I tested this on myself: "I design content models before I pick a platform..."
- The Salsify test: "In July a recruiter brought me a role managing product data..."
- What a keyword filter actually measures: "That one came with a reason."
- What I would change in the job description: "Ask for the work, then name the tools as examples."
- Closer: "Menus take a month to learn. The problem underneath them took me a career."

A 30-second reader recovers the thesis and the ask. Two first sentences do no skim work: "Here is one job in three platform types." and "That one came with a reason." (it points backward).

**Length:** about 1,100 body words by hand count (Studio's counter is the authority), inside the 400 to 1,200 article band. No section is over budget. If it needs trimming, the first debt is signposting ("This one is about what the requirements section of those postings has started to look like.") and the proof-of-concept section.

It is budgeted, not over-extended, and no off-theme image sits in a slot it owns.

## What worked

- "The vendor supplies the buttons." Compression plus a concrete image; carries the whole proxy argument in five words.
- "Learning the menus takes weeks. Learning the problem takes years." The parallel earns the closer's callback.
- "Not 'no PIM'... Not 'can't govern product data'. The category was fine. The brand was the problem." Concede the two readings, then name the real one. Candidate for the Do This table.
- "I'm not arguing this one was unfair." The concession buys the credibility the rest of the section spends.
- "That is a theory, and I am labelling it one." Names the limit of the evidence without hedge-stacking.
- "I was a noodlehead in Photoshop for years." Voice; the word does what "extensive experience" cannot.

## Notes to the writer (no edit proposed)

- **The filter theory is a theory, and an ATS may not match strings at all.** The article labels it a theory, which is honest. Modern screening tools may use more than keyword matching; "matching strings" is the weakest sentence in the piece if a recruiter reads it. Your earlier post and the ST-162 research are the place to source it.
- **Terminology drifts:** "job posts", "job descriptions", "postings", "requirements sections". Pick one for the argument and keep the others for rhythm.
- **Diagram:** it renders readably in the local preview (checked 2026-11-10); the two columns start at different heights, which is cosmetic. It has a caption but no description beyond the box labels; a screen reader reads the labels, which carry the content.
- **"Five years in a specific DAM / Hands-on with one particular headless CMS":** these read as what postings say. Confirm they reflect postings you saw, or say they are composites.
- **The closer repeats section 2's last lines on purpose.** It is a callback, not a repeat; keep it.
- **Identifying details of the personal case are deliberately absent** from the article (no company, recruiter, agency or rate).
