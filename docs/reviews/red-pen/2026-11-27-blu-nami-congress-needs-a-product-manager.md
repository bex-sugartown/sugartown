## Reviewer independence

The draft was written in the same session that invoked this review, which the skill
flags: an editor reviewing its own draft grades too kindly. Bex chose the recommended
split. Passes 2, 3, 3.5 and 4 were run by a **fresh-context subagent** with no memory of
writing the draft; the orchestrating session separately built the PM artifacts and the
heading change Bex asked for by name.

Pass 1 (accuracy) was skipped deliberately: every factual claim was verified against
primary sources earlier the same day and the citations record the method.

**The independence earned its cost.** Six of the fourteen findings are defects in prose the
orchestrating session wrote and had already defended once in conversation, including one
outright factual error (#9), one internal contradiction between two sections (#10), and one
banned-vocabulary miss (#1) that the session's own compliance script had passed because it
only grepped a subset of the banned list.

## Editor's verdict

Close to publishable, and the strongest draft in the folder. The central move, reading a
political slogan as an unwritten requirements document, is load-bearing rather than
decorative: the piece runs the same discovery on four separate objects, a vendor name, a
slogan, a cost model, and its own roadmap. What holds it back is not argument or voice but
three repetitions a piece at its word ceiling cannot afford, plus one contradiction. The
closing callout carries almost all the debt.

## Narrative map

**Thesis:** A fight that looks like a disagreement about policy is a disagreement about a
product name; writing down the five requirements the name stands in for shows the
disagreement is smaller and more tractable than the slogan makes it look.

**Beats:** hook (§1 Dynamic Media) → tension (§2 the slogan fight) → escalation ×3 (§3
Backroads, §4 the Sanders spec and the Jeffries gap, §5 the cost measurement) → resolution
(§6 prioritisation and the author's own error) → resolution 2 (§7 the roadmap) → aftermath
(§8 the fact-check callout). No beat missing, none out of order.

**Length:** 2,491 prose words against a 1,200–2,500 long-form band. At the ceiling. The
fourteen findings are net subtractive by roughly 70 words.

| Structure | Rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis clarity | strong | Stated in the title, restated as mechanism in §2, proved in §4 |
| Beat completeness | strong | Full arc, three escalation stages, aftermath has a job |
| Momentum | adequate | §5 carries one 250-word paragraph; that is the bail-out point |
| Payoff | strong | The closer spends what §1 earned; slightly dulled by the ask being pre-spent in §5 |
| Skim integrity | strong | Headings alone carry the argument |
| Length economy | adequate | At the ceiling; the waste is duplication, the cheap kind to fix |
| Theme discipline | strong | One controlling metaphor, deployed at the beats, not taxed everywhere |

**Controlling metaphor:** product development applied to legislation (product name,
requirement, discovery, PRD, roadmap, release, exit criterion, blocker, sprint window). It
survives heavy use because the metaphor *is* the thesis rather than decoration. Theme budget
recommendation: deploy no further.

## What worked

- **"The ACA passed in March 2010. Democrats lost the House that November. A majority is a sprint window, not a condition."** Two facts and a reframe, no connective tissue; the reader does the inference.
- **"Same chamber, same morning, identical tallies, opposite bills, both short of the sixty they needed and both sunk by the same absent senator."** Does a table's work better than a table, because the point is the symmetry rather than the numbers.
- **"Nobody argued with me, which is the honest limit of the story: it is one person's platform."** Volunteers the weakness in its own evidence before anyone can raise it. Candidate for the brand guide's Do This table.
- **"That is the Dynamic Media mistake, committed by me, in this draft, after spending the first half of this piece warning about it."** The demonstration and the confession in one sentence.
- **"That sentence is a requirements document wearing a lab coat."** Converts a wall of Urban Institute prose into evidence.

## Notes to the writer (no edit proposed)

- **"Five claims" is a number the reader cannot audit.** The callout's own paragraph supports three or four. The open-questions block shows there were more corrections than it lists, so five is defensible but not demonstrated. Name the fifth or drop to a supported count.
- **§2 and §4 first sentences report rather than claim.** A deliberate trade in both cases, since the headings carry the claim. Worth not making it a third time.
- **"Nine days after that segment" appears verbatim in §4 and §8.** At the ceiling, the callout can say "Nine days later".
- **The historical "we" in §1 is fine.** A real team, not corporate plural. No finding.
- **The first-person contract holds throughout.** One to watch: if the "over a hundred runs" count came from a session rather than your own reading, the article's own standard argues for crediting it.

**Render check against the real renderer, 2026-11-27.** Run on Bex's instruction, after the
above was written. A harness at `apps/web/st218-harness.html` reproduced `PageSections.jsx`'s
exact `mermaid.initialize` config (securityLevel strict, theme base, elk, htmlLabels, the
dark-theme token palette) and rendered all three diagrams at the 760px detail-page width.

**All three diagrams as first written were wrong for this renderer.** Checked against the two
proven diagrams on `/platform`, which use neither:

| What was written | House pattern | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `<br/>` for line breaks | `\n` | `securityLevel: 'strict'` escapes HTML in labels, so the tags would have printed literally |
| `<b>` for emphasis | none used anywhere | Same |
| `classDef` with hex fills | none used anywhere | Bypasses the token layer and breaks dark mode, where the renderer computes a separate palette |

Fixed, then re-rendered. A second defect only the render showed: with `htmlLabels: true`
mermaid re-wraps label text, so manual `\n` breaks inside long labels produced orphan words
("one", "produce" alone on a line). Labels were shortened to two or three short segments,
matching the house pattern, and both flowcharts now render cleanly with no orphans and no
tag leakage.

**The Gantt fails and should not ship.** It renders without throwing, but: task labels
overlap their bars, the "Majority may end" milestone text collides with its diamond, the bars
are grey rather than Pink Moon because the renderer's `themeVariables` are flowchart-specific
and set none of the gantt theme variables, and it overflows the 760px column. It would also
be the first gantt in the repo; the config block has no `gantt` section and its own comment
scopes retesting to "the 3 /platform diagrams", all flowcharts.

**Pre-existing condition noted, not caused by this work:** the config sets
`flowchart.defaultRenderer: 'elk'` but no `@mermaid-js/layout-elk` package is installed, so
elk routing is probably not actually in effect. Worth its own issue.
