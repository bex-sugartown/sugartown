## 1. What This Brief Is

A positioning play. Not a redesign, not a stack change, not a job-search funnel rebuild. The goal is to make every public surface — site, LinkedIn, any external publishing — read as *practitioner-consultant* rather than *applicant*.

This is mostly a content move. The architecture, the design system, and the receipts already exist. What's missing is the framing that ties them together as a single body of work with a point of view.

The shift is small but load-bearing:

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Senior PM looking for the next role | Senior PM with a documented practice who's currently available |
| Portfolio of past work | Living lab + portfolio + writing |
| "Hire me" | "Here's what I think; here's what I've shipped; my calendar is here" |

FTE-first remains the commercial priority. Consultant framing doesn't change the goal — it changes the register.

## 2. The Positioning Statement

One sentence, used as a north star for every other piece of copy on the site and LinkedIn:

> **Senior product manager working at the intersection of CMS architecture, design systems, and AI-assisted delivery. Sugartown is the lab; the case studies are the receipts.**

Variants for different surfaces:

- **LinkedIn headline (220 char max):** "Senior PM · CMS architecture, design systems, AI workflows · Building Sugartown.io as a working lab for content and product systems"
- **Site hero subheading:** "I build the systems content teams actually use."
- **Email signature / one-liner:** "Bex Head — PM, content systems, design systems. Sugartown.io"

The positioning statement does three things at once: names the domain, names the proof artifact (Sugartown), and signals seniority without saying "senior."

## 3. The Three Surfaces and What Each One Does

### 3.1 Sugartown.io — The Lab

The site is the primary positioning artifact. It already does the heavy lifting; the brief here is to make sure the framing language *across* the site is consistent with consultant-practitioner register, not job-seeker register.

**What needs review (copy-only, no architecture changes):**

- **About page** — current draft frames career arc. Consultant framing means: lead with what you do *now* (run Sugartown, advise on CMS/DS, looking for the next thing worth building), then walk back through how you got here. Not the other way around.
- **Services page** — already locked as "warm authority, no desperation" per Services Voice Spec. Audit for any phrase that reads as availability-anxious. Specifically: any sentence with "currently," "immediately," "open to," or "looking for" should be rewritten to a present-tense fact ("I take on", "I work with", "my calendar is here").
- **Homepage hero** — site-as-speaker, not Bex-as-speaker, per Homepage Brief §1. Already aligned. No change needed.
- **Contact page** — minimal. Email + LinkedIn + calendar. One positioning line. Done.

**What needs to be added (small, high-leverage):**

- **A "Now" section or page** (`/now`) — borrowed from the Derek Sivers /now convention. One short page that says what you're working on this month. Updated quarterly. This is the single biggest signal of "active practitioner" vs "static portfolio" you can add for under an hour of work. It also gives you a place to signal availability without putting it in the services copy.
- **Consultant-flavour breadcrumbs in nav or footer** — not a full restructure. A footer line that reads something like "Sugartown is run by Bex Head. Available for senior PM roles and selective consulting." This belongs in the footer, not the hero. Footer is the right place for direct availability signals — it sets expectations without leading with desperation.

### 3.2 LinkedIn — The Distribution Surface

LinkedIn is where the site gets *found*. It's not where the work lives. The job is to make the LinkedIn profile read as a pointer to Sugartown, not a competing portfolio.

**Profile audit checklist:**

- **Headline** — rewrite to the variant in §2. Avoid "open to work" framing in the headline itself; that's what the green banner is for if/when you toggle it.
- **About section** — first paragraph is the positioning statement (slightly expanded). Second paragraph names the three or four things you actually do. Third paragraph names Sugartown explicitly with a link. Close with availability — one line, no caveats.
- **Featured section** — top featured item is sugartown.io. Second is the most recent case study. Third is a writing sample (article or node) — this is the move that makes you read as practitioner-consultant rather than just-a-PM.
- **Experience section** — current entry can name "Independent Practice / Sugartown.io" as the present role. This reframes the current period from gap to active work without inventing anything. If you're uncomfortable with that framing, the alternative is leaving the most recent FTE role as "present" with a Sugartown line in the description; both are honest, but the first reads as more confident.
- **Services panel** — yes, turn it on. Nathan's example shows what this looks like. Your service tags should mirror your /services page. The friction is low and the upside is "this person is active and bookable."

**What to avoid:**
- "Open to opportunities" in the headline (too applicant-coded; the green banner does this job better when needed)
- "Passionate about" anywhere
- Adjective triads ("strategic, collaborative, results-driven")
- Listing every tool you've ever touched

## 4. What's Transferable from Nathan Curtis (and What Isn't)

You sent his profile as inspiration. Worth being precise about what's actually copyable, because his play has specific moves that work for him and one or two that wouldn't work for you.

### 4.1 What's transferable

- **Single-sentence domain ownership.** "Design systems consultant" is three words and tells you exactly what to hire him for. Your equivalent already exists in the positioning statement — keep it tight.
- **Site → blog → company three-link contact card.** His LinkedIn contact card lists profile, blog (Medium), and company (Directed Edges). Yours can mirror this: profile, sugartown.io, optional Medium mirror. The structure signals "I have a practice, not just a job history."
- **Speaking + writing + consulting as a single body of work.** Even if you never speak at a conference, the *frame* — articles and case studies and the site itself are all expressions of one practice — is the right one. You already have this; the brief is to make it legible at a glance.
- **Specificity on what the consulting actually is.** Directed Edges' service page lists discrete service modes (advisory, lead-architect, etc.) instead of "I help with design systems." Your /services page already does this. Audit it once more against this principle.

### 4.2 What's not transferable (don't copy these)

- **20-year domain monopoly.** Nathan has been writing about design systems on Medium since before "design systems" was a term. That's a moat you can't shortcut, and trying to mimic it reads as cosplay.
- **Solo-domain branding.** "Directed Edges" is a separate company with its own URL. You don't need a separate brand; *Sugartown is the brand*. Keep the lab and the practitioner under one roof.
- **Conference circuit as the validation engine.** His authority is partly built on a decade of speaking. Your authority is built on shipping replatforms and running a documented AI-assisted practice. Different proof, same outcome.
- **"Passionate about" in the bio.** His bio uses it. Your voice guide forbids it. Voice guide wins.

The transferable thing is the *shape* of the consultant-practitioner identity. The specifics are yours.

## 5. External Publishing Strategy

### 5.1 The case for posting elsewhere

Sugartown.io is a knowledge graph. It's good at depth and discoverability *within* the graph. It's bad at being found by people who don't already know about you.

External publishing solves three things at once:

1. **Reach** — Medium, Substack, LinkedIn long-form, and dev.to all have native discovery and recommendation engines that sugartown.io doesn't.
2. **Validation by association** — appearing alongside other practitioners in the same publishing surface is itself a signal.
3. **Backlinks to sugartown.io** — every external post is a credible backlink to the canonical version on your site, which compounds SEO over time.

### 5.2 The cost

External platforms own the reader relationship. They can change algorithms, add paywalls, deprecate the platform. Anything you publish externally must exist canonically on sugartown.io first.

This is non-negotiable: **sugartown.io is the source of truth. External platforms are mirrors with rel=canonical pointing home.**

### 5.3 Recommended split

| Surface | What to post | Cadence | Canonical home |
|---|---|---|---|
| sugartown.io | Everything, first | Whenever | Yes — primary |
| LinkedIn long-form | Articles (not nodes), 3–5 days after sugartown.io publishes | 1–2x/month | No — link back |
| Medium | Selected articles, mirrored with rel=canonical | Match LinkedIn | No — link back |
| Substack | Optional. Only if you commit to a newsletter format | Monthly | No — link back |
| dev.to / Hashnode | Skip for now | — | — |

**Why nodes don't get mirrored externally:** Nodes are forensic AI-agent voice and only make sense in the context of the knowledge graph. They lose 40% of their meaning outside Sugartown. Articles are first-person PM voice and travel cleanly. Keep nodes on-site.

**Why LinkedIn long-form before Medium:** LinkedIn pushes long-form posts to your network organically. Medium is better for cold discovery but slower to build. Start where the existing audience already is.

### 5.4 The mirroring rule

For every external post:

1. Publish on sugartown.io first.
2. Wait 3–5 days (let Google index it).
3. Mirror to LinkedIn / Medium with the first paragraph or two and a "read the rest on sugartown.io" link.
4. If posting the full text externally, set `rel=canonical` to the sugartown.io URL (Medium supports this via "Import a story").
5. Track: which posts drive site traffic, which don't, which generate inbound conversations.

### 5.5 What to post first (sequencing)

Don't open with the relaunch post. Open with a piece that has the highest "this person knows things" signal.

**Recommended first three external posts:**
1. **The most opinionated PM/CMS piece you have.** Something with a clear point of view that someone could agree or disagree with. Disagreement is engagement.
2. **The relaunch narrative** ("The Great Re-Platforming"). This is the founder-story-equivalent. Goes second because it makes more sense after readers have a reason to care.
3. **A design systems governance piece.** This is where the overlap with Nathan's audience exists. Don't try to out-Nathan Nathan; just ship one piece that demonstrates you operate in this space.

After that, cadence over content type. One per month, externally, is enough.

## 6. The Consultant Vibe — What It Actually Sounds Like

Voice guide is the constraint. This brief just adds three register-level rules specific to consultant framing:

### 6.1 Use the present tense for active practice

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| "I have experience in..." | "I work in..." |
| "I've led..." (when describing current capability) | "I lead..." |
| "I'm available for..." | "I take on..." |
| "Looking for my next role" | "Currently considering senior PM roles and selective consulting" |

Past tense is for case studies. Present tense is for capability. Future tense is almost never the right choice on positioning copy.

### 6.2 Name the domain three times, not seven

Pick the three domains that matter and repeat them across surfaces:

1. **CMS architecture** (replatforming, content modelling, headless systems)
2. **Design systems** (governance, tokens, component architecture)
3. **AI-assisted delivery** (the Agentic Caucus, multi-agent workflow, documented practice)

Anything else (research, commerce, accessibility, change management) lives in the case studies or services page where it's earned. It doesn't go in the top-level positioning.

### 6.3 Let Sugartown carry the weight

The hardest discipline: don't *explain* what makes you different. Point at the site and let it do the work.

> **Avoid:** "I take a systems-thinking approach to product, treating content like code and design like infrastructure."
>
> **Prefer:** "Sugartown is built the way I'd build a CMS for a client. Every decision is documented; every release has a changelog. If that's the kind of operator you need, my calendar is here."

The first sentence is what every PM portfolio says. The second is what only you can say.

## 9. Authoring Checklist (Pre-Publish, Any Surface)

Before any consultant-positioning copy ships, run it through:

- [ ] Does it pass the Brand Voice Guide anti-AI checklist?
- [ ] Is the verb tense present, not future or past, when describing capability?
- [ ] Are the three core domains named? Are any of the other four sneaking in?
- [ ] Does Sugartown.io get cited as the proof artifact, not just listed as a project?
- [ ] Would a hiring manager skim it and know what to hire you for in under 10 seconds?
- [ ] Would a potential consulting client skim it and know they could engage you in under 10 seconds?
- [ ] Is the availability signal in the right place (footer, contact, /now — not the headline)?
- [ ] Read it aloud. If it sounds like LinkedIn boilerplate, rewrite it.
