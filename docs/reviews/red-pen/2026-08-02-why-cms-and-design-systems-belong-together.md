## Editor's verdict

The thesis is strong and the voice is genuinely Bex: "Different languages, same physics" is the whole piece in four words, and the Storyblok pilot story (arbitrary tool choice turned into a portability proof) is exactly the show-the-receipts register the tone spectrum asks for. The weak link is the one that matters most for this brief: the article's atomic mapping (Tokens = Atoms, Content Blocks = Molecules, Templates = Organisms) contradicts both the Brad Frost source it links and the deck that is about to illustrate it. Publishing slides next to prose that tier the same nouns differently would recreate, inside one article, the exact vocabulary problem the deck opens on. Fix the mapping, sweep the seven em dashes, and the piece is publishable with its personality intact.

## Narrative map

**Thesis:** Content models and design systems are the same modular idea spoken in two dialects; give them one shared vocabulary inside one composable ecosystem and you stop paying for replatforms and redesigns.

**Beats:** Hook (two worlds, one physics) → concept coined (Composable Content & Design Ecosystem) → stakes (Why This Matters) → case 1, CMS side (Estée Lauder Storyblok pilot: setup → decision → outcome, complete) → case 2, DS side (beauty retailer personalization: setup → decision → theory-only outcome, honestly admitted) → tactics → big-picture closer. One beat is underbuilt: the shared vocabulary itself never gets a proper artifact in the article — three loose bullets carry it. The deck now does this beat better than the article does.

**Skim skeleton:** "Oh, oh… Atomic: CMS and Design Systems 4evar" / hero: "Oh, oh… Atomic" + "CMS and Design Systems 4evar" / Overview / Why This Matters / How It Plays Out in CMS / How It Plays Out in Design Systems / Tactics for a Composable Ecosystem / The Big Picture / closer: "…that's when digital experiences scale beautifully." A 30-second reader gets the thesis and the arc; headings carry meaning.

**Length:** 990 words vs the ~1,600-word article band. Under budget, no debt.

## What worked

- **"Different languages, same physics."** Compression at its best; the controlling metaphor in one beat. Do This table candidate.
- **"(reader, we did)"** — the parenthetical aside is dry, earns the payoff of the portability argument, and is unmistakably a human writing.
- **"The design brings it to life, but the system keeps it alive."** The contrarian claim ("system" > "design") delivered as a memorable chiasmus.
- **Deck: "Status: exploring" on the title slide** matches the DS status-token vocabulary (`--st-status-exploring-*`). Platform-coherent detail.
- **Deck: the plainness argument** ("deliberately the plainest set" in slide 03's speaker notes) is the right instinct and matches the house instruction-writing style.
